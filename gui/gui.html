Virtual cables

  

    
Welcome to BananaSplit. One optional step before you start: virtual cables. You can skip it, and come back with Menu › Virtual cables… at any time.

    
Looking for cables…

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    

    


    
What they are for

    
A virtual cable is an audio device that only exists in software. It gives a BananaSplit channel a device of its own:

    
    
      
  • On a strip it is an output device. Pick it as the speaker in a game, a browser or Discord, and that app’s sound has a strip of its own.
    • 
      
  • On a bus it is a microphone. Pick it as the microphone in Discord or OBS, and they hear that bus’s mix.
    • 
    

    
Two kinds of cables work:

    
    
      
  • Splitter, BananaSplit’s own: a cable for every channel, named after it. Apps play into “Splitter 1” … “Splitter 6” to reach strips 1 to 6, and record “Splitter A1” … “Splitter A3” to hear buses V1 to V3 (named A1 to A3 before 2.8.25: the cable keeps that name so apps find it), “Splitter B1” and “Splitter B2” to hear B1 and B2 (once the bus’s cable is on in its menu; B1 and B2 have theirs on to begin with).
    • 
      
  • VB-Audio’s cables (VB-CABLE: CABLE, CABLE-A … CABLE-D). Each pair serves one channel, strips first, then buses: “CABLE Input” is the output device, “CABLE Output” the microphone. BananaSplit uses them for the channels Splitter doesn’t cover.
    • 
    

    
Without cables, everything else works: your headset and other devices, the mixer, recording, playback and VBAN.


    
With Voicemeeter

    

    
Splitter and VB-CABLE add devices of their own and leave Voicemeeter’s (Voicemeeter Input, Voicemeeter AUX …) and its settings as they are. BananaSplit never uses Voicemeeter’s devices as its cables, and only connects to one if you pick it in a channel’s menu.

    
To pass sound between Voicemeeter and BananaSplit without installing anything, use VBAN (the VBAN button at the top). On the same computer only one of them can listen on UDP port 6980, so give BananaSplit another port there (6981, say) and send to 127.0.0.1.


    
Your default devices

    

    
BananaSplit never changes the Windows defaults. When a new audio device is installed, though, Windows may make it the default, so after installing a cable check here, or in the Windows sound settings, that your headset is still the default. Apps would otherwise play into the cable instead of your ears. (A bus on “Default output”, B2 to begin with, skips a cable either way, so the mixer never feeds itself.)


    
Installing Splitter

    
For now, Splitter is a test build: Microsoft hasn’t signed it yet, so Windows only loads it in test-signing mode. That mode needs Secure Boot off, and some games’ anti-cheat refuses to run in it. If that matters to you, use VB-Audio’s cables until Splitter is signed.

    
    
      
  1. Open the Splitter download page, download the Splitter zip and extract it.
    2. 
      
  2. Switch test signing on as its README.txt explains (once), then double-click install.cmd and allow it to make changes.
    3. 
      
  3. Start BananaSplit. It finds the cables by itself and uses them instead of VB-Audio’s, so point your apps at the Splitter cables; check your default devices here.
    4. 
    


    
Installing VB-Audio’s cables

    
    
      
  1. Open the VB-Audio download page and download the VB-CABLE driver pack (free). CABLE-A … CABLE-D, for more channels, come in two extra packs (A+B, C+D) on the same page.
    2. 
      
  2. Extract the zip, right-click VBCABLE_Setup_x64.exe and choose Run as administrator, then Install Driver.
    3. 
      
  3. Restart Windows: the driver needs it.
    4. 
      
  4. Start BananaSplit. It finds the cables by itself; check your default devices here.
    5. 
    

    
BananaSplit doesn’t install drivers itself: that takes administrator rights and the driver’s own installer, and you decide whether you want them.

    

    


    
What they are for

    
A virtual cable is an audio device that only exists in software: what an app plays into it comes out of its input. It gives a BananaSplit channel a device of its own:

    
    
      
  • On a strip it is an output device. Pick it as the output in a game, a browser or Discord, and that app’s sound has a strip of its own.
    • 
      
  • On a bus it is a microphone. Pick it as the microphone in Discord, Zoom or OBS, and they hear that bus’s mix.
    • 
    

    
Each cable serves one channel whose cable is on: strips first, then buses. A channel keeps the cable it has, also when others are switched on or off, and its menu shows which one it is. BananaSplit finds these cables by their names:

    
    
      
  • BlackHole (free): “BlackHole 2ch”, “BlackHole 16ch” and “BlackHole 64ch” are three cables; install as many as you need.
    • 
      
  • VB-Audio’s VB-Cable, Rogue Amoeba’s Loopback (its “Loopback Audio” devices) and the older Soundflower.
    • 
    

    
Without cables, everything else works: your headset and other devices, the mixer, recording, playback and VBAN.


    
Your default devices

    

    
BananaSplit never changes the macOS defaults. A newly installed cable can become one, though: after installing, check here or in System Settings › Sound that your headset is still the default output and input. Apps would otherwise play into the cable instead of your ears. (A bus on “Default output”, B2 to begin with, skips a cable either way, so the mixer never feeds itself.)


    
Installing BlackHole

    
    
      
  1. Open BlackHole’s download page and install it with its installer, or with Homebrew in the Terminal: brew install blackhole-2ch (or blackhole-16ch, blackhole-64ch).
    2. 
      
  2. Click Check again: BananaSplit finds the cables by itself. (If a cable doesn’t show up, restart the Mac.)
    3. 
      
  3. Check your default devices here.
    4. 
    

    
BlackHole is a program of its own, with a licence of its own (GPL-3.0): BananaSplit only uses its devices. BananaSplit doesn’t install drivers itself: that takes an administrator’s password and the driver’s own installer, and you decide whether you want them.
